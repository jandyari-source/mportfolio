About me

Operations is where a business wins or loses.

I'm a junior from New Carrollton, Maryland, studying Industrial & Operations Engineering in the Honors Engineering Program. My path to IOE started with a vintage clothing business I ran on Depop. Shipping delays frustrated my customers, and I was spending more on sourcing clothes than I made selling them. That showed me that operations is where a business wins or loses. Through NSBE and IISE, I found a field built around fixing exactly those problems.

Today I'm VP of Recruitment for the Michigan Engineering Consulting Group, a business analyst with Michigan Innovation Ventures, and an MLT Consulting Fellow. I'm a native English and Spanish speaker, and above all I value connection: my best work comes from teams that genuinely enjoy working together. My goal is to become a leader in consulting, solving hard operational problems while building teams people want to be part of.

Class projects

IOE 461: Six Sigma Case Study

Quality Engineering · Fall 2026

  • Risk Management
  • Systems Thinking

Our 4-person team used DMAIC on an accounts payable department losing about $470K a year to late payments, rework, and missed discounts.

  • Analyzed 8 months of records and 6,954 invoices in Minitab (ANOVA, regression, control charts).
  • Found the causes were in the process, not staffing: a first-in, first-out queue missed 62.8% of early-payment discounts.
  • Recommended a due-date queue and a $15 exception cutoff. Projected on-time payment rose from 75.6% to 98%, about $376K saved per year.
  • Named the $90K worst case and planned audits and a two-region pilot to control it.

Learned: A good recommendation names its own risks.

IOE 373: Bank Loan Campaign

Data Analytics · Winter 2026

  • Communication
  • Ethics

Built a logistic regression model on 5,000 customers to predict who would accept a personal-loan offer, after a campaign with about a 9% acceptance rate.

  • 95.8% accuracy on held-out data; 89% of flagged customers accepted (172 of 194).
  • Turned the top predictors (income, education, CD accounts, family size) into a targeting plan.

Looking back: The model missed about a third of likely acceptors, and inputs like ZIP code could steer offers unfairly. Accurate isn't the same as fair.

ENGR 100: Curing the Healthcare System

Operations Engineer · Feb–Apr 2025

  • Systems Thinking
  • Empathy
  • Teamwork

Applied operations engineering to getting injured patients seen on time at Michigan Medicine's Sports Clinic.

  • Led a 4-person team building a Python (SimPy) discrete-event simulation of scheduling under unpredictable demand.
  • A 56-slot schedule raised on-time access from 87.3% to 94.2% and cut projected cost from $350K to about $277K.
  • Presented to stakeholders as a consulting-style poster.

Learned: Behind every scheduling percentage is a patient waiting for care.
